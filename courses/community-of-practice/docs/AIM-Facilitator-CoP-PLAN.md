# Build it. Light it. Show it.
### AIM Facilitator Community of Practice — 45 minutes — Session & Interactive Workbook Plan

**Version:** 1.0 · 11 September 2026
**Delivery:** three separate 45-minute runs — Brisbane (in-room), Sydney (in-room), Online (cohort)
**Audience:** AIM facilitators — peers, not learners. Experienced in the room, mixed in their AI confidence, all of them now delivering virtually at least some of the time.
**Mode:** hands-on. Laptops open throughout. Everyone builds something.
**Destination:** `AIM Interactive Workbooks/courses/community-of-practice/`
**Pattern:** single-page companion workbook (not the multi-page module build) — this is a 45-minute session, and the workbook should read as a bench, not a curriculum.

---

## 1 · The spine

Three parts risk landing as three unrelated things. They need one argument holding them together, stated at the top and paid off at the end:

> **The specialist gap has closed in both directions.** Building a working tool used to need a developer. Looking broadcast-quality on a call used to need a studio. Neither is true any more — and facilitators are exactly the people who should notice first.

That gives the session a shape rather than a running order:

| Part | What they do | What changes for them |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Build it** | Write an Act–Explain–Please brief, paste it into Google AI Studio, watch a working app appear | *I can specify software in English* |
| **2 · Light it** | Learn three-point lighting, audit their own camera, fix one thing live | *I can look like I take this seriously* |
| **3 · Show it** | Tour finished builds, remix one, leave holding a link | *This is not a toy — I have a thing I'll use on Tuesday* |

**Working title:** *Build it. Light it. Show it.* ✦
Alternates: *Ten Minutes to Something Real* · *The Facilitator's Unfair Advantage* · *Make · Light · Share*

**The one-sentence promise, said at 0:01 and tested at 0:45:**
*"In forty-five minutes you'll have built a working tool, fixed how you look on camera, and left with a link you can use with your next cohort."*

---

## 2 · Why this works as a Community of Practice (and not a webinar)

A CoP earns its name if facilitators leave with something *of each other's*, not just something of yours. Three deliberate moves:

1. **Everything built in the room gets shared** — Part 1 briefs go into the chat / workbook wall; Part 3 links go on the same wall. The wall persists across all three runs, so the Sydney cohort sees what Brisbane made.
2. **Two of the three parts are practice problems, not content** — "what tool do you wish you had for your next cohort" and "what's wrong with your current camera setup" are both questions only they can answer.
3. **The session ends by asking what the next one should be.** Non-negotiable. Two minutes, in the workbook, captured.

## 3 · The run sheet

Total 45:00 · 12 / 15 / 12 + 6 for open, transitions and close.

### Cold open — 0:00–2:00 (2 min)

**The hook (do not skip, and do not replace with an icebreaker).** You appear on screen badly lit — overhead office light, camera below eye level, back to a window. Hold it for fifteen seconds while you welcome them. Then cut to your proper setup mid-sentence. Say nothing about it. Let the room react.

Then the promise, the workbook URL and the access code on screen. Nothing else. No agenda slide — the workbook *is* the agenda and they're looking at it.

> **Facilitator note:** this cold open does three jobs — it wakes the room, it pre-sells Part 2, and it proves the point of the whole session before you've made an argument. In-room runs need this as a pre-recorded clip or a second camera; online it's a live source switch.

---

### PART 1 · Build it — 2:00–14:00 (12 min)

**Act · Explain · Please, then a real build.**

| Time | Beat | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 2:00–4:00 | **Why most prompts fail** | They're a wish, not a brief. AEP in ninety seconds, using AIM's existing canon so it's reinforcement, not a new framework: **Act** (who the model is) · **Explain** (the situation, the audience, the constraints) · **Please** (the specific ask and the format). One weak prompt, one AEP rewrite, side by side. |
| 4:00–7:00 | **Hands-on 1 — the brief** | *"Think of one tool you wish you had for your next cohort."* Three fields in the workbook's **AEP Brief Builder**. Three minutes, timer on screen. The builder assembles their answers into a paste-ready app prompt. |
| 7:00–8:00 | **Two out loud** | Two or three read theirs. This is the peer-learning beat — they'll steal each other's ideas, which is the point. |
| 8:00–13:00 | **Hands-on 2 — the build** | Copy button → Google AI Studio **Build** → paste → watch. Five minutes. Most will have something running inside ninety seconds; the rest of the time is them poking at it. |
| 13:00–14:00 | **Name the shift** | *"You just specified software in English and it got built. Six months ago that sentence was a conference keynote."* Hold it there — don't teach the code view, don't mention deployment. That's Part 3. |

**Seeded ideas** (on screen during the three minutes, for anyone who blanks): a random group allocator that won't put the same people together twice · a debrief question wheel for your subject · a countdown timer with the AIM brand on it · a scenario card generator · a "what would you do" branching dilemma · an end-of-day feedback form that summarises itself.

**Fallbacks, pre-built in the workbook:**
- A **Copy this instead** button with a known-good prompt, for anyone whose brief stalls.
- A **live link to a finished version of that same prompt**, for anyone who can't get into AI Studio at all. They watch, they don't drown.

### PART 2 · Light it — 14:00–29:00 (15 min)

**Three-point lighting is the spine. The Rodecaster is the encore. Softboxes are a take-away, not stage time.**

| Time | Beat | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 14:00–15:30 | **Why bother** | You are on camera more than you are in a room now. People decide something about your competence in the first two seconds, and it isn't fair, and it is still true. Lighting is the single highest-return fix — ahead of camera, ahead of background, roughly level with audio. |
| 15:30–20:00 | **The three lights, added one at a time** | The money moment. Live model (you, or a volunteer on a projected feed). **Dark → key only → + fill → + back.** Name each as it lands: **Key** — your main source, 45° off-axis, slightly above eyeline, big and soft. **Fill** — opposite side, weaker, kills the harsh shadow without flattening the face; a white wall or a sheet of foamcore does this for free. **Back/rim** — behind and above, lifts you off the background. Then take the key away and leave the overhead on, so they see the raccoon-eyes version they're probably broadcasting right now. |
| 20:00–23:00 | **Hands-on 3 — the 60-second audit** | Everyone's camera on (online: gallery view; in-room: laptop cameras, Photo Booth or the meeting preview). Workbook checklist, six items. **Fix one thing** before the timer ends. Then look at the gallery again. |
| 23:00–26:00 | **The kit ladder** | Four honest tiers — see §5.4. The message is *tier 1 is free and gets you 70% of the way*, not *buy a light*. |
| 26:00–29:00 | **The encore — Rodecaster Video S** | Three minutes. **Show, don't teach.** Three HDMI sources and a USB-C camera into one box; it presents to Teams or Zoom as a single webcam and microphone, so you cut between your face, your slides and a document camera from physical buttons instead of screen-sharing. Five scene buttons with layered graphics and lower-thirds, automatic chroma key, auto-switching that follows whoever's talking. End on: *"This is where it goes if you get obsessed. You don't need it. I wanted you to see the ceiling."* |

**The six-item audit checklist** (workbook, scored):
1. Is your main light **in front of you**, not behind? (Window at your back is the number one killer.)
2. Is the camera at **eye level**? (Laptop on books. Free.)
3. Is there **one dominant source**, or three competing ones?
4. Is the **overhead light off**? (It lights the top of your head and hollows your eyes.)
5. Do your sources **match in colour** — all warm or all cool, not one of each?
6. Is there **any separation** between you and the wall behind you?

> **Venue note — this part plays very differently across the three runs.** Online is the strongest: everyone is already framed, so the audit is live, visible and slightly competitive. In-room you lose that, so the volunteer demo has to carry more, and you'll need a camera on the projector. Plan the in-room version as *demo-heavy, audit-light*; the online version as *demo-light, audit-heavy*. Same content, different centre of gravity.

### PART 3 · Show it — 29:00–41:00 (12 min)

**The gallery, then a remix, then the wall.**

| Time | Beat | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 29:00–33:00 | **The gallery tour** | Six finished builds, roughly thirty seconds each. Each card carries **what it's for**, **how long it took**, and **a live link**. Move fast — the effect is cumulative, not individual. |
| 33:00–39:00 | **Hands-on 4 — remix or iterate** | Two doors, their choice: **(a) Remix** — open a gallery build in AI Studio and change one thing so it's theirs (the questions, the subject, the branding). **(b) Iterate** — go back to their Part 1 build and give it one improvement. Six minutes. This is the longest uninterrupted working block in the session; stay quiet and circulate. |
| 39:00–41:00 | **The wall** | Everyone pastes their link. Thirty seconds scrolling what the room made in six minutes. This is the emotional peak — do not rush it and do not talk over it. |

**Gallery curation — the single most important content decision in the plan.**

Your existing vibe-coded pieces are mostly games (Snake, Space Invaders, Two-Player Tetris, Sudoku, Asteroids, Scissors Paper Rock). Games prove *it's easy and it's fun*, which matters. But this audience is being asked to teach this and to use it — so a gallery of only games quietly tells them it's a novelty.

**Recommended mix: 2 games + 4 tools.**

| # | Slot | Candidate | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Game — the "you built *that*?" opener | **Two-Player Tetris** or **Space Invaders** | ✅ exists |
| 2 | Tool — genuinely useful, unglamorous | **QR code generator** (rupertchesman.com/qr) | ✅ exists |
| 3 | Tool — teaching-specific | **PDF read-aloud app** | ✅ exists |
| 4 | Tool — facilitator-specific | Group allocator / debrief wheel / branded timer | ⚠️ **build before the first run** |
| 5 | Tool — data or workflow | An AIM Interactive Workbook extra, or an n8n demo | ✅ exists |
| 6 | Game — the closer, played by the room | **Snake** or **Scissors Paper Rock** | ✅ exists |

Slot 4 is the one worth building fresh, because it's the one that makes a facilitator think *I could have that by Friday*. Budget an hour.

### Close — 41:00–45:00 (4 min)

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 41:00–42:30 | **One commitment each.** Typed into the workbook, kept: *"Before my next session I will ___."* Three or four read out. Specific beats ambitious. |
| 42:30–43:30 | **What the workbook holds for later** — the lighting reference, the kit ladder, the gallery, the AEP card they can put in front of their own learners. Thirty seconds, not a tour. |
| 43:30–45:00 | **What should the next CoP be?** Captured in the workbook. This is what makes it a community rather than a broadcast — and it gives you the brief for round two for free. |

## 4 · If it runs long — the agreed cut list

It will run long. Decide the order now, not at 0:38.

| Cut | Saves | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Rodecaster encore** → becomes a workbook panel and an offer to stay back | 3:00 | Low. It's an encore. |
| 2. **Gallery from 6 cards to 4** | 2:00 | Low. Cumulative effect survives four. |
| 3. **Part 3 remix from 6 min to 4** | 2:00 | Medium. Shortens the best working block. |
| 4. **"Two out loud" in Part 1** | 1:00 | High — this is the peer beat. Cut last. |

**Never cut:** the cold open, the three-lights-added-one-at-a-time demo, or the wall.

## 5 · The Interactive Workbook

### 5.1 Shape

**One page.** `courses/community-of-practice/index.html` — three stations, a pre-flight strip at the top, a keep-this section at the bottom, and a facilitator layer behind `?facilitator=1`. Everything in one deployable file, matching the pattern used for the Extras builds.

This deliberately breaks the multi-page module pattern used for the eight-module courses. A 45-minute CoP that opens onto a course map would over-promise. If the lighting reference proves to be the thing people return to, promote it to `pages/lighting.html` afterwards.

```
courses/community-of-practice/
├── index.html              ← everything
├── assets/
│   ├── css/site.css        ← inherited tokens + CoP accent
│   ├── js/site.js          ← capture engine, gate, facilitator mode
│   ├── img/                ← AIM logos
│   └── pics/               ← lighting diagrams, setup photos, Rodecaster still
└── docs/
    └── AIM-Facilitator-CoP-PLAN.md   ← this file
```

**Hub integration:** a card on `AIM Interactive Workbooks/index.html`, tagged **`Community of Practice · 45 minutes`** so it visibly isn't a course. Access code **`cop2026`** (follows `lead2026` / `agent2026` / `team2026` / `leaders2026`).

### 5.2 Design system

Inherit the AIM workbook system — IBM Plex Sans / Sans Condensed / Mono, the cream and ink grounds, the sticky header, Self-paced ↔ Present toggle.

**Differentiator:** where Responsible AI Leadership leads teal and Agentic AI for Leaders leads ink + coral, this leads **AIM blue with a warm amber accent on a cream ground** — lighter, workshop-bench, peer-to-peer. It should feel like a handout at a meet-up, not a module.

### 5.3 The pre-flight strip

Sits at the very top, visible from the moment they open the link, and is the answer to the session's biggest risk.

> **Before you arrive — 5 minutes**
> ☐ Sign in to **Google AI Studio** and open Build mode. **If your AIM account blocks it, use a personal Google account** — find that out now, not at 0:08.
> ☐ Laptop charged, camera working, headphones if you're online.
> ☐ Think of one thing you wish existed for your next cohort.

Send this with the calendar invite as well as in the workbook. **The single biggest predictable failure in this session is twenty people discovering a corporate Google Workspace restriction simultaneously at minute eight.**

### 5.4 The seven interactive pieces

| # | Piece | What it does | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **AEP Brief Builder** | Three fields (Act / Explain / Please) reframed for *"a tool for my cohort"*; live-assembles a paste-ready AI Studio prompt with a Copy button | Adapt the proven assembler from AI Essentials Module 1 — same mechanic, new framing. Saves locally. |
| 2 | **The Lighting Rig** ★ | The centrepiece. Plan-view dial with three toggleable lights around a head; toggling updates a live preview. Presets: *Overhead only · Window key · Key only · Key + fill · Full three-point*. Each preset carries a one-line verdict. | SVG + layered CSS, no photography — projector-safe, loads instantly, reads as premium. This is the piece people will come back to and the piece they'll show their learners. Budget accordingly. |
| 3 | **60-Second Self-Audit** | Six checkboxes, live score, plus a *"the one thing I'm fixing"* capture field | Runs live in Part 2 and again at home. |
| 4 | **The Kit Ladder** | Four expandable tiers: **Free** (window + white wall + books under the laptop) → **Entry** (one LED panel with diffusion) → **Serious** (two lights + softbox + stand) → **Broadcast** (Rodecaster Video S and friends). Each tier states what it actually buys you. | **Confirm AUD pricing at build time** — don't ship indicative numbers. |
| 5 | **The Gallery** | Six cards: live link, what it's for, how long it took, **Remix this** button | Per §3 curation. |
| 6 | **The Wall** | Shared paste-your-link board, persisting across all three runs | Needs shared state — see §7. Falls back to meeting chat if that's a step too far. |
| 7 | **Facilitator layer** | `?facilitator=1` reveals the run sheet, per-venue notes, the cut list, and the fallback links | Same pattern as the course builds. |

Plus the **commitment card** and the **next-CoP question** at the foot of the page.

### 5.5 What's needed before it can be built

- **Slot 4 gallery build** — a facilitator-specific tool that doesn't exist yet (≈1 hour).
- **Lighting stills** — your actual setup, lit and unlit; the bad-lighting cold-open frames.
- **Rodecaster photograph** — one good still of the console in use.
- **AUD pricing** for the kit ladder tiers.
- **Decision on the Wall** — shared state or chat fallback.

## 6 · The three runs — what differs

Same design, three sets of notes in the facilitator layer.

| | **Brisbane (room)** | **Sydney (room)** | **Online** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Part 1** | Wifi is the risk. Confirm guest network capacity for 20 laptops in advance. | Same. | Easiest — everyone's on their own connection. |
| **Part 2 demo** | Needs a volunteer + a camera feed to the projector, and the lights physically present. **Confirm what travels to Brisbane** — a reduced two-light demo is fine and may be wiser than freighting the kit. | Home ground. Full kit. | Strongest version — you *are* the demo, live, no extra hardware. |
| **Part 2 audit** | Laptop cameras; harder to make visible to the room. Demo-heavy, audit-light. | Same. | Gallery view makes it collective and slightly competitive. Demo-light, audit-heavy. |
| **Part 3 wall** | Chat is weaker in-room — use the workbook wall or a projected QR to it. | Same. | Meeting chat works fine. |
| **Energy risk** | Room goes quiet during the six-minute build. Circulate; don't fill the silence. | Same. | Screens go dark and you can't tell if they're working. Ask for a reaction at the three-minute mark. |
| **Run order** | Pilot here if it's first — it's the smaller room. | | Run last if possible; it benefits most from the first two rehearsals. |

## 7 · Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **AIM Google accounts block AI Studio** | **High** | Pre-flight strip in the calendar invite; personal-Gmail instruction; watch-only fallback link in the workbook. |
| Venue wifi can't carry 20 laptops building | Medium | Confirm with each venue; have the finished-example links ready so the session survives a dead network. |
| AI Studio rate limits or slows with 20 simultaneous builds | Medium | Stagger by seeding six different example prompts rather than one. Fallback links again. |
| Part 2 overruns — it's the richest part and the most fun to talk about | **High** | Hard timer on the Rodecaster encore. It's cut #1 for a reason. |
| Kit doesn't make it to Brisbane | Medium | Decide the reduced-kit version now and rehearse it, rather than improvising. |
| Facilitators disengage during hands-on ("I'll do this later") | Medium | The wall. Nobody wants to be the one with nothing on it. |
| Shared wall needs infrastructure you'd rather not build | Medium | Ship with the chat fallback; add the wall in v1.1 if the sessions want it. |

## 10 · The honest note

Three substantial parts in forty-five minutes is ambitious, and the plan above is built to survive that rather than pretend otherwise: one argument holding it together, a pre-flight strip that moves the friction outside the session, four hands-on blocks that are short enough to finish, and a cut list agreed in advance.

The part most likely to break is Part 1 — not because the content is hard, but because twenty people signing in to a Google product at the same moment is a coin toss. Everything in §5.3 and §7 exists to make that a non-event.

The part most likely to be remembered is the three lights going on one at a time.
