## Casting & representation notes (apply to every prompt)

- The set varies **ethnicity, gender, age and body type** — Aboriginal Australian, Torres Strait Islander, Māori, Samoan, Filipino, Vietnamese, Chinese-Australian, South Asian, Lebanese-Australian, Black African and Caribbean, Latino, and white subjects; women, men and non-binary presentation; ages ~28 to ~68; slim, average, plus-size, tall and short builds. One wheelchair user and one person with a hearing aid appear incidentally, both as people with agency rather than as illustrations of disability.
- **Authority is distributed.** Across the set, the person running the room, demonstrating the craft, being asked the question or making the thing is by turns a plus-size Māori woman, an older East Asian man, an Aboriginal Australian woman, a Filipino man in his 50s and a young Lebanese-Australian woman. Nobody is only ever the observer.
- These are **facilitators and trainers**, not executives. Dress them as Australian corporate trainers actually dress — smart, comfortable, layered, lanyards, few jackets, no ties, good shoes because they stand all day.

### The lighting-specific harm, and how this set handles it

This workbook is *about lighting*, which makes one trap unavoidable and worth naming. Film stock and, later, camera auto-exposure were calibrated for pale skin for most of a century — so an image set that depicts "badly lit" as a dark-skinned face and "beautifully lit" as a pale one reproduces exactly that history, however unintentionally.

Two rules hold across the set:

1. **The "bad lighting" frames contain no people.** They show the *setup* — the empty desk under an overhead fluoro, the chair with its back to the window, the laptop on a low table. The fault sits with the room, which is also where the fault actually sits.
2. **Darker skin is lit with visible craft.** Every prompt featuring a Black, Aboriginal, South Asian, Pacific Islander or Māori subject specifies exposure *for the subject*, with full tonal detail and luminance in the skin. The set's most beautifully lit images are of its darkest-skinned subjects — which is both the corrective and, frankly, the better photograph.

## Production notes

- **Generate 3–4 variations of each and cull hard.** The portraits in section D are the ones worth re-rolling until they're right — they carry the teaching, and a mediocre lighting portrait in a workbook about lighting undermines the whole page.
- **Images 9, 10 and 11 must read as a progression.** Generate them in one sitting, keep the backdrop, framing and crop consistent, and accept only a set where the difference between them is legibly the lighting and nothing else. If Midjourney won't hold consistency, shoot these three yourself — you have the kit, and a real progression beats a generated one here.
- **Images 12 and 13 are a matched pair.** Same desk, same angle, same focal length. Generate 12 first and use it as a reference for 13.
- **Nothing in this set needs a person's face at high resolution except the section D portraits and image 7.** Everything else survives at 1600px wide, which keeps the single-page workbook fast on venue wifi.
- **Skin tone check before you ship.** Open the final set side by side and confirm the darker-skinned subjects are the best-exposed images in the folder. If they aren't, re-roll — that's the whole point of the second casting rule.
