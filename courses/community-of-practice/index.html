Why we're here

The argument

The specialist gap has closed in both directions. Building a working tool used to need a developer. Looking broadcast-quality on a call used to need a studio. Neither is true any more — and facilitators are exactly the people who should notice first.

Three stations, forty-five minutes, and you build something in each one.

The framework you already teach

Act · Explain · Please

A weak prompt is a wish. A good one is a brief — and a brief is now all it takes to get working software.

  • ActWho the model is. A facilitator's tool-builder, a curriculum designer, a front-end developer who writes plain, readable code.
  • ExplainThe situation, the audience and the constraints. Who uses it, where, on what, and what must not happen.
  • PleaseThe specific ask and the format. Not "help me with" — the actual thing you want handed back.

Hands on

You are on camera more than you are in a room now. People decide something about your competence in the first two seconds, it isn't fair, and it is still true. Lighting is the highest-return fix you can make today.

Full three-point

The kit ladder

Tier one is free and gets you most of the way. Everything above it is a refinement, not a rescue.

Tier 1A window and some books$0

Turn your desk so you face a window. Put the laptop on a stack of hardbacks until the camera is at eye level. Turn the ceiling light off. Put anything white — a sheet of foamcore, a whiteboard, an open lever-arch file — on the shadow side to bounce light back.

That is a key light and a fill. You have just built two thirds of a three-point setup out of furniture.

Cost: nothing. You already own all of it.

What it won't doWork after dark, or on a grey day, or if your only window is behind you and can't be moved.
Tier 2One LED panel with diffusion≈ $200

A small bi-colour folding panel on a clamp arm or a short stand, with its diffusion clipped on. Set it 45° off-axis and slightly above your eyeline, then turn it down — the most common mistake with a first light is running it flat out.

Bi-colour matters more than power: it lets you match the window in the afternoon and the room lamp at night, which is audit question five.

Benchmark: a Godox Litemons LP800Bi 80W bi-colour panel is about $199. Anything in that class does the job.

What it won't doSeparate you from the wall behind. That's tier three.
Tier 3Two lights, a softbox and stands≈ $550–750

A proper softbox as the key — bigger source, softer shadow, that's the whole physics of it — plus a second smaller light behind and above you for the rim. Fill can still be a bounce board; it doesn't need to be a light.

This is the tier where people stop noticing your lighting and start noticing you, which is the actual goal.

Benchmark: a 70cm Godox parabolic softbox is about $139, plus a second light and two stands. Around $550–750 all in, depending on how good the lights are.

What it won't doFit in a bag. Once you're on stands you're committing floor space.
Tier 4A production console≈ $930–1,250

The point at which lighting stops being the interesting problem and switching does. See the encore below.

Benchmark: the RØDECaster Video S lists between $1,095 and $1,250 depending on the retailer, and has been selling around $930–990. The original RØDECaster Video was more than twice that.

What it won't doMake an unlit face look good. Nothing downstream fixes what the lights didn't do.

The encore · three minutes

Where this goes if you get obsessed

The RØDECaster Video S is a small console that takes three HDMI sources and a USB camera, and presents all of it to Teams or Zoom as one webcam and one microphone.

Which means you stop screen-sharing and start directing: cut from your face, to your slides, to a document camera, from physical buttons.

  • Three HDMI inputs plus USB-C, with automatic frame-rate conversion
  • Five scene buttons carrying layered graphics and lower-thirds
  • Automatic chroma key, so a background goes without a green screen ritual
  • Auto-switching that follows whoever is talking
  • Two mic inputs with processing, plus built-in wireless receivers
You don't need it. I wanted you to see the ceiling.
If you remember one thing

One soft light, in front of you, slightly above your eyes, and the ceiling light off. That's ninety per cent of it, and none of it costs anything.

Hands on · 6 minutes

Now make one of them yours

The longest working block of the session. Two doors — pick either, there's no wrong one.

One change, not five The instinct is to rewrite everything. Resist it — ask for one specific change, look at what comes back, then ask for the next. That habit is the actual skill, and it's the one worth teaching your own cohorts.

If you're remixing: open the gallery link, hit Remix in AI Studio, and it forks into your own copy. Nothing you do touches the original.
