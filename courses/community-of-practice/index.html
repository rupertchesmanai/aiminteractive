Act · Explain · Please

Most prompts fail because they're a wish, not a brief. A brief says who the model is, what the situation is, and exactly what you want handed back.

The same ask, twiceA wish, and a brief

The wish
Write me a case study about giving feedback.

Eight words. No role, no audience, no length, no format. Whatever comes back is generic by construction — and then we blame the model.

The brief
You are a learning designer at the Australian Institute of Management. I'm running a 20-minute activity for first-time managers on giving feedback. Australian workplace, plain language, no jargon, no American spellings. They'll read it on paper in pairs. Write a 400-word case study with one realistic dilemma and three discussion questions. Plain text, no headings.

Three paragraphs, three jobs. The model now knows who it is, what room it's in, and what done looks like.

ActExplainPlease
The framework AIM already teaches

Act · Explain · Please

  • ActWho the model is. A learning designer, a plain-English editor, a front-end developer who writes readable code. The role sets the standard it reaches for.
  • ExplainThe situation, the audience and the constraints. Who this is for, where it will be used, and what must not happen.
  • PleaseThe specific ask and the format. Not "help me with" — the actual thing you want handed back, with its shape and its length.

Context Windows

A model can only work with what's on the desk in front of it. The desk is big these days — but it is a desk, not a filing cabinet, and what slides off the edge is gone.

Cutoff Dates & Training

A model is trained on a snapshot of the world that ends on a date. It is released months later. You use it months after that. Everything in that gap, it has never seen — unless you let it search.

How a model learnsTrained, released, used

The Model Cutoff Clock

Which models know what, and until when

Pick a model. The timeline shows where its knowledge stops, when it was released, and how far "today" has drifted from both. Dates shift every time a new model ships — the live list is here.

Live on screenSearch off. Search on.

Ask it this, twice
What has happened in Australian workplace news this week?
Open a chat ↗
Web search off

It can only use the snapshot.

One of two things happens, and both are the lesson. It tells you it can't know — good. Or it answers confidently from months-old data without saying so — the one that gets people in trouble.

Web search on

It goes and looks.

Now it reads today's pages and cites them. Same model, same question. The difference is whether you let it leave the snapshot.

Hold this

If the answer depends on anything after the cutoff — a price, a policy, a person's job title — turn search on, or don't trust it.

Cutoff dates meet context windowsMake sure it's got the context — considering the cutoff date

Ask first

Could it have seen this?

If the thing you're asking about is newer than the cutoff — or it's yours, like a facilitator guide — the model has never seen it. No amount of good prompting changes that.

Then supply it

Put it on the desk.

Paste the pages, attach the document, turn search on. Context is how you give a model what training couldn't — and it only works if you actually do it.

And check

Did it use what you gave it?

Ask it to quote the page. If the answer sounds plausible but cites nothing you supplied, it's answering from the snapshot, not from your context.

And one more thing it was never trained on Your course. Your facilitator guide, your activity numbers, your running case. A model has never seen any of it, which is why Section 2 starts by putting it on the desk.

The rule worth stealing

Nobody is only ever the junior.

Generative tools default to a type. If you don't write the room you want, you get the room the training data imagines — and your learners will notice before you do.

What you'll see

Phase after phase, each with a backup.

Modules 2 to 8, the plan page, references, the cheat-sheet slot — one phase at a time, a .pre-pN.bak beside every file it overwrites, tests after each.

What you do

Nothing.
