Module overview

What AI is, really

An AI model is a system trained to recognise patterns in data. A large language model (LLM) is built by compressing enormous amounts of text into a predictive model. It doesn't "think" — it predicts the most likely next word, or token.

LLMs are built during a training phase and have a cutoff date — they have no knowledge of events after that date and can't access the internet in real time unless you explicitly turn on web search. That single fact explains a huge amount of AI's odd behaviour.

The key line to hold throughout: AI models predict patterns based on training data — they do not truly "know" or "understand." They have no awareness, and no idea what happened after their cutoff date.

Key takeaways

AI is not magic. It's pattern prediction — myths arise from misunderstanding, and the truth is AI augments human work.
Clear prompts shape results. Context is everything — Act, Explain, Please beats a vague one-liner every time.
Cutoff dates matter. AI has no knowledge of events after its training — turn on web search when it counts.
A bit of fun helps you learn what can go right, and what can go wrong, without workplace stakes.
