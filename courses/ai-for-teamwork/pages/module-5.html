The problem

Why teams struggle with analysis.

People fall back on familiar patterns, preferred methods or functional expertise. Important context gets overlooked, risks are understated, and recommendations end up weaker than they appear. Multiple lenses reduce the risk of false certainty — especially under pressure.

AI can support broader thinking, but its output requires careful guidance, verification and correction. Accuracy, completeness, and spotting what still needs work remain human responsibilities.

Widen the thinking

Six Thinking Hats.

Edward de Bono's method separates emotions, facts, risks and ideas into distinct lenses — reducing confusion, lowering conflict and producing more balanced thinking. Tap a hat for its lens and a ready-made AI prompt stem.

Use the Hats when: teams are stuck in one perspective; conversations mix emotion, detail, speculation and strategy; a decision feels rushed; stakeholders disagree because they're using different thinking styles without realising; or analysis needs to slow down so assumptions can surface. The method pairs well with an LLM: the model generates ideas or questions per hat, the team judges which insights are relevant and accurate.

DISC-informed lenses.

A behavioural-preference view: explore the same issue from four working-style angles, so both people and process factors get examined.

D · Dominance

Fast-paced, results-first — what decision moves this forward now?

I · Influence

People-oriented — who needs to be engaged, energised, brought along?

S · Steadiness

Task-oriented and consistent — what keeps delivery stable through the change?

C · Conscientiousness

Methodical — what does the evidence, detail and process actually say?

Diagnostic frameworks.

Where the Hats and DISC widen how you think, these structure what you analyse. AI can generate questions for each lens — humans decide which questions are appropriate.

PESTLEPolitical, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental — external influences on your project or organisation.
SWOT + evidenceStrengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats — each point backed by evidence, not vibes.
VRIOValue, Rarity, Imitability, Organisation — can your capabilities sustain competitive advantage?
McKinsey 7SStrategy, Structure, Systems, Skills, Staff, Style, Shared Values — diagnosing alignment issues and collaboration barriers.
Activity 5.1

AI for balanced thinking and analysis

A deliberately stress-inducing scenario. Each team member uses an LLM with a different risk or diagnosis framework — then you compare what each lens surfaced. AI must surface gaps, not resolve them.

The scenario — workload pressure across five strategic projects
Scenario: workload pressure on teams
Your organisation is running five major strategic projects: Atlas (customer portal rebuild), Helios (regulatory reporting uplift), Meridian (data platform migration), Northstar (branch operations redesign) and Riverstone (cybersecurity strengthening). Together they are a substantial part of the strategic roadmap — and they compete for the same constrained pool of talent: 38 key delivery roles, but only 24 full-time staff. The gap is filled by part-time contractors with unpredictable tenure, creating fluctuating capability in solution architecture, data engineering and test management.

Over the last three months, reported workload rose 27% while resourcing capacity rose only 6%. Leading indicators of strain: sick leave up from 1.8 to 3.1 days per person per month; informal overtime normalised; usually-clear staff submitting incomplete, ambiguous work needing multiple rework cycles; inconsistent attendance at cross-team alignment sessions. Atlas is five weeks behind schedule. Helios has two compliance defects found by internal audit — both traced to rushed verification and inadequate peer review.

Despite this, leadership dashboards still present a positive picture: green milestones, on-track budgets. Many risk logs haven't been updated for seven to ten weeks; PMs report being "too busy delivering" to run risk reviews. Teams are declining optional training, deferring mandatory access reviews and deprioritising continuous improvement. Executives receive polished decks while teams describe mounting pressure, unclear priorities and resource conflicts.

Shortcuts are compounding risk: sprint items merged without acceptance criteria met; stakeholder validation compressed or skipped; change communications drafted without impact assessments. Helios and Meridian are in friction over the same two critical data engineers. Senior staff are cancelling planned leave, reinforcing a culture of heroic effort over sustainable delivery.

These patterns — rising workload, reduced quality signals, shortcuts, stale risk information, resource friction, and a widening gap between governance reporting and reality — suggest systemic strain. Without intervention: missed regulatory obligations, reputational damage from delayed releases, burnout in key roles, and expensive rework.

Steps

  • Each team member asks an LLM to suggest one or more risk/diagnosis frameworks (e.g. McKinsey 7S) and applies a different one.
  • Craft prompts so the LLM engages you to gather more information — never invents facts absent from the scenario.
  • Have the AI suggest missing information and blind spots — you may invent details to feed it, as humans (in real life, you'd add real context).
  • Compare outputs across frameworks. What did multiple lenses catch that one would have missed?
The no-invention guard — add this to your prompt
You must not invent, infer, or assume facts.
If you cannot complete the task using only the provided information:
- Say: "Insufficient information to proceed."
- List the exact information required.
- Ask clarifying questions.
Producing a confident answer with missing data is considered an error.

Your insights

What did the frameworks surface? Where did AI help you get to grips with a stressful scenario — and where did it risk doing the thinking for you?

Discussion questions

  • How did AI alongside a framework change how quickly you got oriented, versus the framework alone?
  • Where did AI help you apply the framework rigorously — and where did it risk doing the thinking for you?
  • What checks ensured the output reflected the scenario, not AI-generated assumptions?

Debrief

  • A single framework both narrows and focuses thinking.
  • Multiple lenses reduce false certainty under pressure.
  • AI is most useful challenging confidence and exposing blind spots — not producing recommendations.
  • This is a stress-inducing scenario by design — debrief how the AI affected participants' grip on it, and any similar experiences.
Culture

AI use and transparency.

Transparency is a powerful risk mitigation — yet most people hesitate to disclose AI use. Why:

Perceived reduced effort or skill"Will they think I didn't put the work in — or can't do it myself?"
Job security anxiety"If AI does this, does my role look more automatable?"
Stigma or cultural resistanceIn some teams and industries, AI use still carries a quiet stigma.
Unclear policiesWhen guidelines on AI use and disclosure are vague, silence feels safer.
The cost of hiding it: hidden reliance means unchecked cognitive offloading; concealed use causes untraceable context drift and undermines accountability; hallucinations go undetected under pressure; and trust erodes as teammates feel excluded from how work is really being done.

Transparent, human-centred teams can…

  • Catch and correct mistakes by openly reviewing AI outputs together.
  • Maintain shared context — e.g. via knowledge files — reducing drift as work moves between people and systems.
  • Develop collective expertise rather than letting skills atrophy.
  • Build trust through honest conversations about how and why AI is used.
Activity 5.2

AI transparency discussion

Reflecting on the workload scenario — its pressure, its hidden shortcuts — discuss the three angles below. The aim is naming the barriers to open dialogue about AI, and how working as a team overcomes them.

Personal reflection

  • How do you feel about being transparent when using AI for work tasks? What concerns or reservations might you have?

Team perspective

  • How does your team view AI use? Any shared beliefs, anxieties, or unspoken norms about disclosing it?

Organisational position

  • What's your organisation's stance? Are policies clear? Open encouragement — or hesitation and uncertainty?
