Ten things you can do with AI to improve your teamwork.

1 · Communication

Rewrite communication for different processing styles

Adjust the same message for analytical thinkers, fast processors, detail-seekers, multilingual colleagues or neurodiverse teammates. Messages land cleanly and reduce follow-up cycles.

Module 3 → 2 · Wellbeing

Tailor check-in messages to strengthen psychological safety

Shape variations of a message to suit different personalities and stress levels. Earlier risk visibility, more open team communication.

Module 3 → 3 · Meetings

Prepare with a structured context summary

Reorganise what you already know — purpose, concerns, expected decisions — into a clearer pre-meeting brief. Sharper discussions, fewer mid-meeting resets.

Module 4 → 4 · Follow-through

Turn messy notes into validated decisions and actions

Group scattered notes, extract decisions, highlight open questions — then verify against the source. Faster follow-through, reliable action lists.

Module 4 → 5 · Thinking

Identify thinking traps and test assumptions

Surface blind spots, alternative interpretations and overlooked risks. Stronger, more balanced recommendations with less cognitive bias.

Modules 4–5 → 6 · Documents

Shift tone or format without losing meaning

Convert long or formal documents into onboarding guides, FAQs, briefing notes or multilingual variants. Clearer understanding, faster knowledge transfer.

Module 7 → 7 · Learning

Make sense of scattered feedback

Feed AI the raw comments from a check-in or retro, organise them into themes — then rewrite the themes yourself. Faster patterns, focused improvement.

Module 7 → 8 · Alignment

Strengthen cross-team alignment with comparison summaries

Side-by-side summaries of multiple viewpoints, using only the information provided. Quicker detection of misalignment and conflicting priorities.

Module 5 → 9 · Consistency

Standardise repeatable processes

Consistent templates for weekly updates, stakeholder messages or incident summaries — checked against your dataset. Less manual effort, more consistency.

Module 7 → 10 · Agents

ContextA note on agents

This course is about generative AI. Agents are a different door.

Although this course focuses on human-centred AI use, it's worth knowing about AI agents — tools that can perform multi-step tasks on your behalf: executing workflows, monitoring a source of truth, pulling information from multiple systems, drafting updates and handing results back to a human for approval.

Different platforms use different names — OpenAI says agents or autonomous tools, Google says agents or automations, Anthropic says tasks or tool-use sequences, Microsoft says Copilot agents. The idea is the same: several steps in a workflow, carried out without you guiding every step in real time.

Used carefully, agents can support teamwork by:

  • Keeping shared documents, trackers or registers up to date
  • Consolidating chat, email and meeting notes into structured summaries
  • Monitoring risks and actions, prompting the team near deadlines
  • Preparing recurring meeting packs or weekly updates
  • Maintaining stakeholder logs and communication notes
  • Assembling onboarding materials automatically
  • Acting as a neutral go-between across teams
Why we're parking them today: errors can compound across an agent's steps, which is why the human-centred techniques in this course come first — master these before "over-automating". Agents are covered in depth in AIM's agentic AI courses; treat this section as post-course reading.

Facilitator cue: "why aren't we using agents?" is the most common early question — point here, keep it brief, and refer interested participants to the agentic AI course. Today's focus is generative AI.

Activity 1.1

Ice-breaker — introduce your team context

A low-stakes first interaction with your LLM: give it real context about how your team works, and ask for your team's "superpower". Early clarity about team operations sets up every activity that follows.

Steps

  • Open your Large Language Model.
  • Prompt it with extensive data on your team — how much co-operation, collaboration and co-ordination the work needs, how shared the goals are, how much communication it takes to deliver value.
  • Ask: based on the kind of work my team does, what is one team superpower we ought to have?
  • Re-prompt to edit the answer into a fun, ice-breaker-friendly summary — 40 words or less.
Example prompt — adapt it to your team
My team is a cross-functional digital learning design group in the education sector. Here is context about how we work:
• We collaborate daily across design, content, facilitation, and quality assurance.
• Our work is moderately interdependent: no single piece can be delivered without handovers and shared context.
• We coordinate closely with SMEs, product managers and delivery teams.
• Communication load is high — we pass work between multiple contributors and must maintain clarity to avoid rework.
• Our shared goals are to produce accurate, inclusive and high-impact learning products.
• We operate in a hybrid environment, so asynchronous clarity matters.
• Common friction points include fragmented updates, assumptions, and uneven visibility of work in progress.

Using only this information, what is ONE "team superpower" my team ought to have to work at its best?

Your team superpower

Place your final answer here — 40 words or less, ice-breaker friendly.

Discussion questions

  • What surprised you about the output you received?
  • What did you immediately trust, and what did you question?
  • What would you need to verify before using this output at work?

Debrief

  • Early interaction lowers barriers to participation and builds psychological safety.
  • AI outputs are provisional and require active human verification.
Activity 1.2

Identify a teamwork challenge to improve with AI

Anchor the day around a real teamwork challenge. If your team already has one, name it. If you want fresh perspective, use your LLM as a thought partner — one prompt to surface improvement areas, a second to explore where AI can help.

Common challenges teams name

  • Inconsistent message interpretation across hybrid teams
  • Slow decisions because inputs arrive fragmented — email, chat, ad-hoc conversations
  • Uneven participation in discussions
  • Workload visibility gaps across projects
  • Manual preparation and follow-through around meetings
Prompt 1 — surface challenges
Here is context about my team and how we work: [describe your team]. Based only on this, list the five most likely teamwork challenges holding us back, phrased as specific, observable problems — not generic categories.
Prompt 2 — where AI can help
For each challenge above, suggest where AI could realistically help — and where it couldn't. Keep human judgement, relationships and accountability out of AI's hands. Flag any suggestion that would need verification or human review.

📌 Your challenge for the day

Capture your final problem statement — one specific challenge, 40 words or less. This follows you through the whole day and returns in Module 8, where you'll design a one-week experiment to improve it.

Discussion questions

  • What makes this challenge persistent rather than one-off?
  • What would visibly change if this improved next week?
  • Where does it show up most often in day-to-day work?

Debrief

  • Well-framed problems improve the usefulness of AI support.
  • Similar challenges often appear across different teams — discuss the clusters.
