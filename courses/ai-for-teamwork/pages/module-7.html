Other Use Cases for AI

Four fast, immediately reusable patterns for the everyday situations where clarity and shared understanding falter: retrospectives, document summarisation, tone transformation, and pro-forma repetitive work.

Cluster retro notes into validated insights — and actions

🕘 ~30 minutes · Activities 7.1–7.4
Use case 1 · The most important for teamwork

Retrospectives and continuous improvement.

Retrospectives make team experience visible and enable deliberate refinement instead of reactive correction. A good retro focuses on insight, not blame. AI helps cluster notes, spot patterns and reframe observations — so the team's attention goes to sense-making and validation, not manual sorting.

Set the stageCreate psychological readiness — everyone present, engaged, willing to reflect.
Gather dataCollect factual and emotional information so the team works from a shared picture.
Generate insightsPatterns, causes, connections — today's exercise lives here.
Decide what to doOne or two concrete, testable improvements — don't overload the team.
CloseReflect on the session; reinforce commitment to the chosen actions.
Activity 7.1

Using AI to support retrospectives

You have Stage 2 notes from a fictional onboarding team's retro — move them to Stage 3 insights using the human-centred workflow, without inventing new information. Download the full notes (29 raw sticky notes) for your LLM.

Warm up by hand — cluster a sample of the notes

Feel the sorting problem before delegating it. Tap a note, then its theme.

Data → insights: three drift-proof prompts

1 · Cluster without adding content
Here is a set of retrospective notes. Cluster them into themes only using the information provided. Do not add, infer or speculate. Return the clustered themes and the notes that belong to each theme.
2 · Identify causes
Using only this list of notes, identify possible causes or contributing factors that explain the patterns you can see. Do not introduce new facts. State your reasoning step by step so I can verify accuracy.
3 · Reframe into insights
Rewrite these clustered notes as clear insights. Each insight should explain what the pattern is and why it matters. Use only the information provided. Do not create new data. Keep each insight to one or two sentences.
+ Insights → actions: three more prompts
1 · Options without new content
Here are the insights we produced. Suggest three possible actions for each insight. Use only the information in the insights. Do not add new problems or new assumptions. Keep each action small, concrete and testable within two weeks.
2 · Prioritise by effort and impact
Using only these candidate actions, classify each into High or Low Effort, and High or Low Impact. Make no assumptions beyond what is written. Recommend the one or two actions that give the team the best balance of low effort and high impact.
3 · Write the action as a commitment
Rewrite the chosen actions as clear commitments. Each should specify: what we will do, who will own it, when we will trial it, how we will know it worked. Do not create any new actions. Use only the selected ones.

Your validated insight

Validate that the LLM invented nothing, then rewrite one insight in your own language to share with the room.

Discussion questions

  • What part of this did AI genuinely reduce effort on?
  • Where did you intervene to stop meaning being added or shifted?
  • Did you "think less" by letting the LLM do most of the exercise?

Debrief & cues

  • AI sorts and spots patterns; it cannot judge accuracy.
  • Insight quality depends on deliberate human checking and rewriting.
  • Participants may need help finding where/how to upload files to their LLM.
  • Slow teams down at validation; redirect if they jump to actions before accurate insights.
Use case 2

Summarisation and reformatting.

Control the instructions tightly

  • Manually identify the key sections or themes first.
  • Give explicit boundaries — "summarise only the following paragraphs", "restructure only Section 3".
  • Specify the exact output format: bullets, a table, a three-paragraph summary.
  • Check output against the source for invention, omission and tone drift.
  • Re-inject source text when accuracy slips; work long documents in sections.
Activity 7.2Optional

Summarise and restructure a long strategy document

Download the sample strategy document (a fictional three-year service transformation strategy). Decide your structure before you prompt — then let the model fill it.

Example prompt series — have fun with this
1. Transcribe this document — only the top-level headings, keep it to 10 or less.
2. Focus on sections 3, 5 and 6; summarise the strategic intent, milestones and investment case in a table.
3. Summarise in this format, focusing on risks: three paragraphs a sceptical board member would actually read.
Debrief: summarisation quality depends more on instruction design than model capability. Clear boundaries reduce invention and unintended emphasis — and human review preserves intent, nuance and decision relevance.
Use case 3

Tone transformation.

AI can shift tone while preserving meaning — but only when boundaries, audience and constraints are clear (style guides, tone-of-voice guides, target personas). Without them, the "AI voice" reads strongly and quality drops. For customer-facing organisations, staying on-brand is critical. Useful for adapting HR documents, policy statements, executive briefings and guidance material for different audiences.

Activity 7.3

Tone transformation of a large body of knowledge

Attach the HR policy document supplied by your facilitator (or reuse the sample strategy document) and transform it for new audiences. Experiment and enjoy the process.

Example prompts
• Transcribe — summarise headings to fifty words or less.
• A new-starter guide, two pages or less: fun, engaging tone for a general audience, with page references to key sections that encourage actually reading the document.
• A 30-question FAQ for general staff use.
• A supplementary 10-question FAQ for staff in the IT department.
• Summarise into "five things we need to know."
• Translate into French and Mandarin.
• Translate into Gen Z speak — fun, approachable, easy for new starters to absorb.
Watch for: tone changes can unintentionally weaken precision or obligations. Clear audience and constraints improve quality — and human rewriting restores credibility and voice.
Use case 4

Pro-forma repetitive tasks.

Fixed inputs, predictable outputs

  • Start with a knowledge file containing only the required fields.
  • Share the template with the AI so it follows the required structure.
  • Insert the data after the template, not mixed into the same message.
  • Verify every line against the raw data before accepting the output.
  • Do the first one or two manually to calibrate.
  • At larger scale, consider projects, agents and custom GPTs.
Activity 7.4

Populate a template using a provided dataset

Have fun with this: download the template and the dataset (12 fictional client onboarding records). Build a minimal knowledge file of just the template fields, then ask your LLM to fill the template from the data.

The template
CLIENT ONBOARDING STATUS SUMMARY — complete one per client, using ONLY information present in the dataset. Do not invent details. Leave a field blank and flag it if the data does not answer it.

CLIENT: [client_name]
INDUSTRY / PLAN: [industry] / [plan]
ONBOARDING MANAGER: [onboarding_manager]
STATUS AT A GLANCE: [health]
KICKOFF COMPLETED: [yes/no] · DATA MIGRATION: [yes/no]
TRAINING PROGRESS: [completed] of [planned] sessions
TARGET GO-LIVE: [go_live_date] · OPEN ISSUES: [open_issues]
ONE-SENTENCE SUMMARY: [plain-English status a busy manager could read in five seconds]
FLAGS / MISSING INFORMATION: [anything the dataset does not tell you]
Debrief: pro-forma quality depends on the knowledge file and template you provide. LLMs are most reliable when inputs and boundaries are tightly controlled — and verification discipline stops small errors becoming systemic.
