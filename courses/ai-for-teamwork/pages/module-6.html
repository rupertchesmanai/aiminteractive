Change Management of AI Adoption

The capstone brings the whole day together: communication tailoring, team types, wellbeing, meeting discipline, structured frameworks and the human-centred workflow — applied in teams to one deliberately complex case. Success is process, not perfection.

The lenses

Change personas — how people meet change.

Reactions to change vary with past experience, confidence, workload and sense of control. Recognising the patterns lets you tailor communication and support — and gives your LLM the context it needs to help meaningfully.

PioneersMove quickly, test ideas early, enjoy ambiguity. They ask for opportunity and autonomy.
PragmatistsAdopt when they see evidence it's useful. They need clarity, examples and low-risk pathways.
ScepticsQuestion assumptions, look for gaps and risks. They need transparency, logic and validated evidence.
TraditionalistsPrefer familiar routines and stable patterns. They need reassurance, clear expectations and time to adjust.
Important: these personas are not labels. They help you anticipate the concerns and motivations that shape behaviour during transformation.

ADKAR — diagnosing where change is stuck.

A practical model for guiding individuals through change: find whether the blocker is awareness, desire, knowledge, ability or reinforcement — then adjust communication or support.

AwarenessDo people understand why the change is needed and what problem it solves?
DesireDo people want to participate and see value in it?
KnowledgeDo people know how to change and what new behaviours are expected?
AbilityCan people perform the new behaviours confidently under real conditions?
ReinforcementAre new behaviours supported, recognised and sustained over time?

Kotter's 8 steps — structuring the whole transformation.

A proven sequence for organisation-wide change — and a grounded framework that makes your LLM's suggestions follow known good practice instead of ad-hoc ideas.

Create urgencyShow why the change matters now.
Build a guiding coalitionLeaders with authority and influence.
Form a strategic visionDefine the future state clearly and simply.
Communicate the visionRepeat it consistently across all channels.
Remove obstaclesAddress barriers in systems, behaviours, policies.
Generate short-term winsVisible early progress builds momentum.
Sustain accelerationUse early wins to drive deeper change.
Institute the changeEmbed new behaviours and norms into everyday work.
The case study

Hospital workforce and timekeeping reform.

A medium-sized hospital network — three campuses, 1,200 clinical staff — has had payroll discrepancies for nine months. Error rates exceed 7%; overtime allocation is inconsistent; staff report underpayment, overpayment and uncertainty; unions have raised formal concerns. Leadership is rolling out Chronos, a digital timekeeping and rostering system, targeting 30% payroll-variance reduction in three months. The timeline is ambitious, consultation has been limited, the system has known gaps, and trust is fragile. This is not only a systems change — it's a behavioural and cultural transformation affecting pay, workload, fairness, safety, trust and industrial relations.

Anti-hallucination rule for the whole capstone: keep a hard line between what the case states and what your team proposes — and actively catch the LLM "completing" missing facts.

SameerIT Systems LeadOwns Chronos configuration; knows the build is incomplete; under pressure to meet timelines.
LeilaWorkforce PlannerNeeds accurate data for forecasts; sees opportunity but fears rushed adoption creates new errors.
Union and industrial relations complexity
Three active unions with different consultation clauses in their enterprise agreements; transformation cannot progress without addressing them. Trust is fragile after historical failures. Union concerns:
  • Chronos may misclassify overtime categories — particularly call-backs and shift extensions.
  • It may reduce pay for blended roles crossing award categories.
  • Managers may use digital timestamps for performance critique or discipline.
  • Chronos training hours aren't included in current staffing allocations.
  • Some ward roster rules don't match the award conditions the system applies.
A previous payroll failure at another state hospital has created a backdrop of caution and strong emotion. Industrial relations risk is high.
Operational challenges and system limitations
System constraints:
  • Rostering templates don't align with specialised ward patterns, including double-coverage procedures.
  • The interface isn't mobile-optimised — yet clinicians work between locations.
  • Automated overtime triggers misinterpret breaks and micro-shifts, over- and under-counting.
  • Award rules for part-time staff with shift extensions apply inconsistently.
Staff behaviours compound the gaps:
  • Paper approvals persist in high-pressure wards and are entered late.
  • Casual staff hold multiple system profiles — duplication and pay errors.
  • Some nurses delay logging overtime out of guilt, fear of rejection or team pressure.
Overtime pressures and financial risk
Overtime is now 12% of total staffing expenditure against an 8% benchmark. Underpayments have damaged trust; overpayments create budget exposure. Executives are concerned about audit findings, legal liability for misclassified entitlements, burnout and clinical safety risks from chronic overtime, and workforce planning crippled by poor data quality. Transformation must stabilise both accuracy and confidence.

Activity 6.1Capstone

Design the transformation response

Up to five teams, each owning one component through its own framework lens. AI may support analysis, option generation and structure — but verify every insight and rewrite it in your own tone. Each output combines later into a single transformation blueprint. Timebox: produce a shareable first version, then spend the final minutes tightening language and checking internal consistency.

Pick your team

Team A · Change & communication strategy

Lens: Change personas (Pioneers · Pragmatists · Sceptics · Traditionalists)
  • A clear narrative for why the transformation is needed
  • Messaging tailored to each persona type
  • A training and adoption outline reflecting different needs
  • A channel strategy for leaders, managers, unions and staff
  • Early communication touchpoints and trust-building moves

Team B · Sequence the change

Lens: Kotter's 8 steps
  • A short diagnosis of where the hospital sits across the 8 steps
  • How to create urgency and build a guiding coalition
  • A draft change vision specific to Chronos and workforce reform
  • Short-term wins targetable in the first four weeks
  • Actions to sustain acceleration and embed the change

Team C · Diagnose transformation barriers

Lens: ADKAR
  • Cultural, behavioural, and workload barriers
  • System and process barriers; industrial relations barriers
  • Gaps in urgency, sponsorship, vision or communication
  • Specific ADKAR breakdowns for key groups

Team D · Operational & system stabilisation

Lens: operations and delivery
  • Known system limitations and configuration priorities
  • Training requirements and capacity constraints
  • Ward-specific rostering patterns; data quality issues
  • Quick wins that reduce errors within four weeks

Team E · Roadmap, risks & measures

Lens: program governance
  • A four-week rapid stabilisation roadmap
  • Major risks with mitigations
  • Measures for accuracy, trust, adoption and overtime stability
  • Dependencies across IT, operations, unions and workforce planning; executive alignment recommendation

Your team's answer

Discuss the outputs in your team and improve the final product — with human knowledge added, the sum of your teamwork should beat the LLM's unthinking analysis.

Facilitator — during breakouts

  • Circulate actively; don't stay at the front.
  • Prompts that force deeper thinking: "What is the AI missing?" · "What needs human judgement here?" · "Does this align with your framework?"
  • Watch for cognitive offloading — intervene when teams look passive.
  • Normalise confusion — the complexity is intentional.

Teaming

  • Small groups (≤6): two teams, A and B. Medium–large: 3–6 teams, one framework each; frameworks can repeat if needed.
  • Fewer teams? Assign from A forwards.
Combining our work

Five outputs, one blueprint.

To bring it together: give the LLM a structure for the final output, have representatives from each team check the fidelity of their inputs in the combined result, and iterate — humans working the outputs, LLMs interrogating them through risk lenses and frameworks as before.

The combining frame — feed each team's output plus this structure
Combine the team outputs below into one coherent transformation blueprint using exactly this structure:
1. Context and current state
2. Barriers and root causes
3. Change sequence
4. Operational stabilisation
5. Communication and adoption
6. Roadmap, risks and measures
Use only the content provided. Where team outputs contradict each other, list the contradiction explicitly rather than resolving it silently.

Delivery pathways: FTF — each team emails their text to the facilitator (simplest and most reliable), who pastes into a master document, merges with the LLM and projects it. Alternatives: QR-code upload folder or a shared doc. Online — each team pastes output into the meeting chat labelled clearly ("Team C — ADKAR section"); facilitator combines, formats and screen-shares. If outputs hit chat word limits, AI can shorten them first.

Whole-group synthesis — the most important step

Discussion questions

  • How did using different frameworks across teams improve the final output?
  • Where did team outputs contradict each other — and why?
  • What detail or nuance was lost when everything was combined into one view?

Debrief

  • Parallel frameworks surface blind spots a single lens misses.
  • Contradictions between outputs are signal — areas needing attention.
  • Combining flattens nuance unless humans actively check for loss of meaning and intent.
