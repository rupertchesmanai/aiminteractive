Team Types & How AI Can Help

"Team" is a catch-all for a spectrum of very different groupings — each with its own demands on communication, clarity and shared understanding. Before using AI to improve teamwork, work out what kind of team you're actually in.

Anchor concepts

Three broad team types.

This course uses three categories to anchor discussion. Most real teams sit somewhere between them — that's exactly what the spectrum activity below is for.

1 · Groups of solo contributors

Most work is largely independent. Individuals progress tasks on their own and connect with others only when sharing updates or handing over work. Errors arise from assumptions, incomplete information or unclear expectations.

Communication load: low — but clarity mattersAI helps: structuring updates, checking logic, refining writing
Typical organisational design
  • A collection of individual contributors with limited structural interdependence.
  • Pools of specialists (analysts, designers, researchers) who deliver work independently.
Typical delivery approaches
  • Classical project management with individual work packages.
  • Kanban-style personal queues or ad-hoc task assignments.
Typical examples
  • Business analysts working separately on different artefacts.
  • Individual contributors in strategy, research or compliance functions.

2 · Loosely connected teams

People work on adjacent or complementary tasks that relate to a shared outcome, but day-to-day progress doesn't depend on continuous interaction. Context can be lost, duplicated or fragmented across channels.

Communication load: moderate, often asynchronousAI helps: unifying information, summarising threads, structuring distributed work
Typical organisational design
  • Pools of shared resources who rotate between initiatives.
  • Functional groups aligned to the same domain but not delivering as a single unit.
Typical delivery approaches
  • Classical project management with individual work packages — especially when average tenure is much shorter than the project.
  • Kanban-style personal queues or ad-hoc assignments.
Typical examples
  • A shared pool of business analysts supporting multiple projects.
  • Cross-functional advisory teams that coordinate occasionally to align outputs.

3 · Shared-goal, high-interdependence teams

Teams in the agile/scrum or core-project sense: members rely heavily on one another's outputs, sometimes hour to hour, and often can't get work done without deep collaboration. Misunderstandings have immediate impact on delivery.

Communication load: high, co-ordination continuousAI helps: meeting prep, synthesising notes, surfacing risks, cutting admin
Typical organisational design
  • Fully cross-functional teams bringing diverse skills together around one unified goal.
  • Stable delivery units operating as a cohesive whole.
Typical delivery approaches
  • Scrum-like approaches with frequent interaction.
  • Continuous-flow Kanban in operations or call-centre teams with high interdependence.
Typical examples
  • Management consulting case teams.
  • Software delivery teams with shared objectives.
  • Cross-functional squads working on a single product or customer outcome.
The pattern

Where AI supports each context.

The communication need varies, but AI supports all three contexts the same way: by reducing ambiguity, structuring information and strengthening shared understanding.

Solo contributors

Refine reasoning, detect gaps, rewrite for clarity, and translate complex work into structured formats.

Loosely connected

Consolidate updates, summarise distributed conversations, and maintain shared visibility across channels.

Interdependent

Clarify objectives, surface missing information, structure action lists, and reduce cognitive load during fast-moving work.

The test for any AI use: AI enhances teamwork when it reduces friction and supports human decision-making — not when it replaces the conversation.
Activity 2.1

Where do my team(s) sit?

Place your team on the five-point collaboration spectrum, and note why. You'll group with participants of similar profiles for later activities — so an honest placement makes the whole day more useful.

Steps

  • Review your team context from Activity 1.1 and your challenge from Activity 1.2 — they'll help you categorise accurately.
  • Drag the marker to the point that best reflects your team's collaboration style.
  • Write a brief rationale for the position you chose.
  • Optionally, ask an LLM to suggest or confirm the placement — then critically review its answer; AI suggestions may be inaccurate or incomplete.

Your rationale

Where do your teams sit — and why did you characterise them as such?

Discussion questions

  • What made this position the best fit for your team?
  • Where did there seem to be disagreement or uncertainty?
  • How might this context change over time or across work?

Debrief

  • Team collaboration context shapes communication needs and failure modes.
  • Correctly identifying context makes later discussions more meaningful.
  • AI outputs require critical review — they remain imperfect even when "completed" and should never replace judgement.
